The problem

They earned daily. Nobody helped them save daily.

In 2022–23, Indian households' net financial savings fell to about 5% of GDP, the lowest in nearly five decades, as borrowing rose. Picture someone earning ₹1,000 to ₹2,000 a day. They want to save, but all they know is a savings account or a fixed deposit at the bank.

Three things held people like them back. We started with gold, the one thing everyone in India already trusts, and tackled each one.

01Make it feel real

Answers: it's confusing

Gold you can't hold is hard to trust

Digital gold can't be seen or touched. And every saver asks the same thing: if I put in a little each day, how much gold will I have?

I proposed answering it visually. A gold coin that grows into a stack as you save, next to the live gold rate and the grams you own.

02Make starting one step

Answers: it's hard to start

₹10 a day, right after sign-up

Management wanted to offer gold straight after OTP, before people had even seen the app. I argued against it. I wanted people to look around first.

I was wrong. Starting straight away worked better: choose an amount, see your gold grow, and pay.

03Make it worth coming back

Answers: nothing rewards saving

Gold Rush: a reason to save again tomorrow

Saving only sticks if people come back. So we introduced Gold Rush, a prize-linked savings game, and I led its design. Banks and governments have used prize-linked savings for decades to encourage saving: your savings stay yours, and saving earns you a chance to win.

Diwali was coming, the season when Indian families traditionally buy gold. I made the case to leadership that Gold Rush should be ready in time. Once it was approved, we had 14 days, starting from scratch. I planned the work, split it across the team, and led the design decisions.

What I learned

Three things I'll take into my next product

  • Users know their own path. I wanted people to explore before investing. They wanted to start right away, and they were right.
  • Think about the whole product. When my mentor left before launch, I had to stop thinking one screen at a time. Decisions got easier.
  • Hear users first-hand. I got research updates from our research team, but never had time to join user interviews myself. That's the part I'd change.
